Coverage and source map

A week-by-week map of the reviewer, slide questions, original materials, and known gaps.

What the study kit covers

Short answer: The beginner reviewer follows the major topics taught in Weeks 1–12, but it is an explanation-first summary, not a line-by-line replacement for every slide. Week 9 has no recording in the supplied folder. The linked chapter slides and Cisco modules cover that gap. The new lecture slide question bank adds all 85 numbered QUIZ slides found in the supplied chapter decks, with each slide's printed answer and source location. The extra concept notes explain details that were too brief in the beginner guide.

How complete is each resource?

  • Beginner lessons: all major week/chapter themes, explained in a readable order. They intentionally leave out some names, standards, historical examples, and slide-specific figures so a new learner can follow the story.
  • Compact reviewer: fast recall sheet for the main protocols, calculations, and comparisons after the beginner lessons.
  • Extra concept notes and slide question bank: detailed terms explicitly tested by numbered slide quizzes, including LLC, controlled access, noise types, asynchronous transmission, ARQ, HTTP request structure, DSLAM, Internet governance, security continuity, and configuration management.
  • Original decks and Cisco modules: full source detail, diagrams, and any figure-only questions. The course-materials catalogue links to all originals and text extractions. The text extractions do not describe diagrams or images.

Important limits

The teacher's revision deck says to review the Canvas chapter quizzes, but those quizzes are not present in this folder. The slide QUIZ questions may resemble exam questions; the deck does not guarantee any will appear verbatim. The revision deck and some transcripts refer to different sittings, so check Canvas for your current exam date, format, and rules.

The answer bank reports the answer printed on each slide, even if wording is dated or unusual. One Chapter 5 slide calls a TCP header “typical” at 192 bits while the same deck gives 20 bytes for the header without options. The extra concept notes explain that distinction. When a source answer seems odd, use the original deck and ask your teacher or compare with the Cisco module before memorizing it.
